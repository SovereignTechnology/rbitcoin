- **Regtest can retarget like mainnet.** `--regtest-block-time 10 --regtest-retarget-interval 60` (conf `regtest_block_time`, `regtest_retarget_interval`) makes regtest run mainnet's difficulty adjustment toward 10-second blocks, every 60 blocks. Either key turns it on; the other keeps mainnet's 600 seconds or 2016 blocks. A miner that adds or drops hashrate moves the difficulty at the next retarget height, so a dev stack can exercise a difficulty change on a CPU. Core's clamp applies, holding the measured timespan between a quarter of the target timespan (rounded down, so a timespan that is not a multiple of 4 can rise more than 4x: 7x at 7 seconds) and four times it, and there are no min-difficulty blocks, as on mainnet. Heights 1 through the first retarget height keep `--regtest-pow-bits` (or the ceiling when unset), since that period would be measured from the 2011 genesis. The target never gets easier than a ceiling that keeps the retarget arithmetic inside 256 bits. Validation, `generate*`, `getblocktemplate` and the SV2 Template Provider all follow it. Regtest only: startup fails on any other network, for a zero block time, an interval below 2, a block time times interval under 4 seconds (the lower clamp would be zero, and a period with no elapsed time would retarget to a target no hash meets), or `--regtest-pow-bits` easier than the ceiling or so hard that its hardest retarget step truncates to zero. Turned on over an existing chain, it applies to new blocks; stored blocks are not checked again. Heights through the first retarget height expect the start bits; above it, blocks easier than the ceiling keep their parent's bits until the next retarget height. This is not a Bitcoin Core option.
